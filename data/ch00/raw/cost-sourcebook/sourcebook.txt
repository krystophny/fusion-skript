# Electricity cost history, 1980–2035

**Solar PV, solar with daily storage, and competing electricity sources — standalone numerical sourcebook\.** 9 October 2026\. No graphics\. Prices are in **constant 2025 US cents per kWh of electricity** unless another unit is explicitly shown\. This Markdown edition reproduces the PDF sourcebook, including all four machine\-readable data tables that were embedded in that PDF\.

**Important:** There is no single observed global price for electricity delivered from solar with sufficient storage to cover 95% of all demand\. We use &#40;a&#41; global project\-weighted new\-solar benchmarks, &#40;b&#41; a synthetic daily battery\-shifting model using global installed storage costs, and &#40;c&#41; IRENA’s separate site\-specific 95%\-**annual\-energy\-coverage** optimisation in Nevada\. US Lazard conventional plants are not the same geographical sample as IRENA’s global renewables\. Historical modelled prices are *not* observations\.

## 1\. Definitions and reproduction instructions

**Electricity LCOE:** lifetime\-discounted costs &#40;capital, operations and fuel if applicable&#41; divided by discounted electricity produced or delivered\. Installed PV price in USD/W, battery storage system capex in USD/kWh of *energy capacity*, and electricity price in US cents/kWh are three fundamentally different physical quantities\.

**Recommended historical logarithmic chart:** plot `year` on X and `us_cents_per_kWh_2025` on log\-scaled Y &#40;suggested ticks 1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300 and 1,000 US¢/kWh&#41;\. Use series named in the annual CSV\. Plot missing values as missing rather than zero\. Draw source\-observed benchmark periods as solid lines with markers, backcast / assumed portions as dashed lines, and 2030–2035 projections separately\. Exclude `oil_fuel_proxy` from full\-LCOE comparisons unless clearly labelled as a proxy\. Do not plot battery capacity prices on the LCOE axis\.

**Price basis:** all the following annual LCOE numbers are approximately converted into 2025 US dollars\. For a nominal USD price observed in year $y$, $P\_\{2025\}=P\_y \\mathrm\{CPI\}\_\{2025\}/\\mathrm\{CPI\}\_y$\. For prices already expressed in 2024 dollars, multiply by $321\.9/313\.689 \\simeq 1\.02617$ &#40;US CPI\-U annual averages &#91;S11&#93;&#41;\. Old OWID component data are rounded values from an earlier extract and may differ from the current source’s deflator or later revisions\.

**Evidence flags** &#40;included with every individual record&#41;: `B` = published benchmark &#40;occasionally rounded/digitised&#41;; `A` = approximate published anchor; `R` = retrospective *model* from a published study; `M` = sourcebook author reconstruction / counterfactual; `I` = interpolation; `F` = future projection/scenario; `L` = midpoint of a published 2025 Lazard cost range; `P` = fuel\-cost proxy, **not** a generating\-plant LCOE\.

**Definition of 95%:** IRENA optimises hourly dispatch to meet 95% of **total annual load energy**\. The denominator is annual demand, not a count of hours fully served\. A 5% annual energy deficit can cluster in reliability\-critical hours\. A 95%\-coverage system is **not equivalent to guaranteed firm supply** or to serving 95% of hours completely &#91;S03&#93;\.

## 2\. Transparent assumptions and methods

### 2\.1 Daily battery shifting &#40;the “average place” scenario&#41;

The reference solar generation cost is the *global 2025 new\-plant average* of 4\.4 US¢/kWh, rather than an invented geographical location\. The daily\-shifting model uses a fraction $f=0\.60$ of **delivered** electricity passing through a battery and 40% direct consumption\. Li\-ion efficiency is $\\eta=0\.85$; lead\-acid scenario $\\eta=0\.75$\. Battery utilisation is $u=0\.95$ equivalent full discharge cycles/day\. Assume a 7% real discount rate, 1% of installed battery capex/year as O&M, and battery life of 15 years &#40;Li&#41; or 5 years &#40;lead&#41;\.

Capital\-recovery factor and delivered\-electricity cost:

$$
\\operatorname\{CRF\}&#40;r,T&#41;=\\frac\{r\}\{1\-&#40;1\+r&#41;^\{\-T\}\},\\qquad
c\_\{\\mathrm\{solar\+daily\}\}=c\_\{\\mathrm\{PV\}\}\\left&#91;&#40;1\-f&#41;\+\\frac\{f\}\{\\eta\}\\right&#93;
\+\\frac\{100 f B&#91;\\operatorname\{CRF\}&#40;r,T&#41;\+m&#93;\}\{365 u\}\.
$$

Here $c$ is in US cents/kWh, $B$ is the **installed usable** battery system cost in 2025 USD/kWh\-capacity, $u=0\.95$, $m=0\.01$, and $f=0\.60$\. The model omits seasonal storage, battery augmentation, energy\-price capture effects, large\-grid reserve requirements, and other system services\.

Installed global four\-hour Li\-ion BESS cost anchors in this history are **$2,571/kWh &#40;2010, originally 2024 dollars&#41;, $192/kWh &#40;2024, originally 2024 dollars&#41;, and $140/kWh &#40;2025, 2025 dollars&#41;** &#91;S01,S02&#93;\. Other 2011–2023 values are modelled/interpolated\. Prior to 2010, the counterfactual daily\-storage curve uses assumed lead\-acid turnkey storage; after 2010 the synthetic model chooses the lower of the Li\-ion and lead scenarios\. This is not a reconstruction of actual system deployment\.

### 2\.2 Backcasting PV electricity costs before 2010

A consistent global PV plant\-LCOE series starts only in 2010 &#91;S02&#93;\. Before 2010, full PV LCOE is derived from the PV\-module price series &#91;S07&#93;, **not measured from actual historical utility plants**:

$$
c\_\{\\mathrm\{PV,pre2010\}\}=100, \\frac\{p\_\{\\mathrm\{module\}\},&#91;\\mathrm\{USD\}\_\{2025\}/\\mathrm W&#93;\\times 1000 \\times M\_y \\times &#40;\\operatorname\{CRF\}&#40;0\.07,25&#41;\+0\.015&#41;\}\{1500\}\.
$$
$M\_y=1\.55\+&#40;1\.85\-1\.55&#41;&#40;y\-1980&#41;/29$ for $1980\\le y\\le 2009$, and the assumed yield is 1,500 kWh per kWp/year\. This is deliberately a transparent **backcast**, not an observed 1980–2009 worldwide PV power\-station LCOE\.

### 2\.3 Early wind, hydro, coal, gas, and nuclear

Onshore wind early benchmarks used for a reconstruction are 12, 8, and 6 US cents/kWh in **2002 USD** for 1980, 1990, and 2000 &#40;historical NREL reference &#91;S13&#93;&#41;\. These are CPI\-U\-normalised to 2025 USD and log\-interpolated to the observed 2010 IRENA wind benchmark\. Hydropower reconstruction anchors &#40;US¢/kWh, 2025 dollars&#41; are **1980 6\.4; 1990 5\.8; 2000 5\.2**, then interpolated to IRENA 2010\.

Lazard historical US new\-build ranges start in 2009 &#91;S04&#93;\. The **non\-observed** 1980/1990/2000 backcast anchors &#40;2025 US¢/kWh&#41; are gas **14\.5/13\.4/11\.5**, coal **11\.5/11\.2/13\.3**, and nuclear **14/16/17**; logarithmic interpolation leads to 2009\. The missing 2022/2024 points within Lazard’s later historical intervals are also log\-interpolations\. **Caution:** a previous exploratory chart accidentally labelled Lazard gas\-peaking values as nuclear; the correct nuclear series is used here\.

**Interpolation rule:** between known positive anchors $&#40;t\_a,p\_a&#41;$ and $&#40;t\_b,p\_b&#41;$, $p&#40;t&#41;=\\exp&#91;&#40;1\-\\lambda&#41;\\ln&#40;p\_a&#41;\+\\lambda\\ln&#40;p\_b&#41;&#93;$, with $\\lambda=&#40;t\-t\_a&#41;/&#40;t\_b\-t\_a&#41;$\.

### 2\.4 Oil\-fired generation: illustrative *fuel proxy* only

Let $W\_y$ be nominal WTI crude oil price in USD/barrel in year $y$ &#91;S12&#93;\. With 1,700 kWh thermal energy per barrel, 33% electrical conversion efficiency, and a constant 5 US¢/kWh non\-fuel allowance, define:

$$c\_\{\\rm oil,proxy\}&#40;y&#41;=5\+\\frac\{100 W\_y\}\{1700\\times 0\.33\}\\frac\{\\mathrm\{CPI\}\_\{2025\}\}\{\\mathrm\{CPI\}\_y\}\.$$

This **is not** an observed worldwide or US new oil\-fired plant LCOE and should not be interpreted as such\. Some pre\-1986 WTI anchors are approximate\. It illustrates sensitivity to oil fuel cost\.

### 6\.1 Pumped hydro daily\-storage counterexample

Illustrative pumped\-hydro daily shifting with installed cost **$156 per usable kWh** for a six\-hour system, 80% efficiency, 50\-year life, 7% real discount rate, 1\.5% annual capex\-based maintenance, and the same 60% fraction of delivered energy shifted yields approximately **7\.4 US¢/kWh** from the 2025 global\-average solar generation benchmark\. This is a scenario, not an observed global pumped\-hydro project price\. Civil works and site geography are decisive\.

## 13\. Critical limitations and interpretation

1. **No uniform global sample:** world IRENA renewable means, US Lazard thermal/fission means, hypothetical average PV daily storage and Nevada’s 95% dispatch model cannot be interpreted as the prices from a single site\. Variability across sites can be larger than differences across technologies\.
2. **Historic pre\-2010 prices are not measured comparable LCOEs:** the 1980–2009 PV generation line is reconstructed from module prices; conventional costs before 2009 use explicitly hypothetical anchors, and early hydro and wind use sparse anchors\. Their graphical slopes are scenarios with significant uncertainty\.
3. **The old nuclear\-series identification was wrong in an earlier figure:** the Lazard gas\-peaking series was mislabelled as nuclear\. This Markdown and the underlying corrected PDF use the 2009–2023 *nuclear* series reported in §5\.
4. **Battery price history is sparse:** 1980–2009 daily shifting uses a hypothetical lead\-acid system\. Many modern installed BESS values between benchmarks are interpolations\. Cell and pack market quotes cannot substitute for turnkey installed\-system costs\.
5. **95% annual\-energy coverage is not reliability:** it permits a 5% annual electricity shortfall, potentially concentrated into a few critical periods\. It excludes some national\-level transmission, weather\-tail, reserve, and other balancing costs\.
6. **Oil is a fuel\-price proxy, not a measured oil plant LCOE\.** The fossil/nuclear new\-build estimates omit unpriced carbon and some externalities\. Existing amortised plants such as the French nuclear fleet have lower but differently defined cost measures\.
7. **The 2030–2035 values are scenarios, not observations\.** In particular, the synthetic global PV\+daily cost follows explicit installed\-capex sensitivity assumptions rather than a direct module\-price extrapolation\.

**Takeaway:** Utility\-scale solar now produces new electricity at roughly **4\.4 US cents/kWh** globally &#40;2025&#41;; a deliberately simplified daily\-shifting model gives roughly **7\.8 cents/kWh**; and an IRENA Nevada 95%\-annual\-energy\-coverage model gives **11\.3 cents/kWh** &#40;2025&#41;, projected to **5\.9 cents/kWh** by 2035\. These three different economic boundaries must not be conflated when evaluating competition with hydro, wind, fossil fuel or nuclear energy\.
